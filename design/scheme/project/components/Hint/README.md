# Hint

The one line of `hint` text at the bottom edge of every window, above a `line-100` rule. At rest it states the window's interaction conventions: verb in `ink`, the rest in `ink-muted`, clauses separated by en dashes. Three clauses at most; if a window needs more, the interaction is too clever. The tips are left-aligned and truncate with an ellipsis rather than shrink; after them comes the `Motion` switch in a window with a `Ground`, and the `Signature` closes the bar on the right. Motion sits at least 16px (`space-4`) before the Signature: the tips give way before that gap does. Neither the Motion switch nor the Signature ever moves.

**Info.** Every control declares what it does in one line of at most 72 characters, written as *Name — what it does* ("Length — 1 to 128 steps; holds on bar lengths, Page Up/Down jumps."). While the pointer is over a control, or the control has visible keyboard focus, the bar shows that line in place of the conventions: the name in `ink`, the dash and the rest in `ink-muted`. It arrives at once and leaves after a 150 ms grace, so the pointer moving along a row of knobs goes from line to line without the conventions flashing in between; while a button is held, the bar keeps the line of what is being dragged. The conventions stay laid out underneath, hidden (`tips stack`, `clauses held`, `info`), so the bar never changes width. A line too long for its window truncates, and the ellipsis says the line is too long. The same line is the control's accessible description, so a screen reader hears what a sighted reader sees; the Motion switch and the Signature have one too. Focus counts only when it is visible: a click focuses a knob as well, and a line that stayed behind after the pointer left would be a stuck bar.

**Precedence:** an action's outcome ("Copied slot 1."), in the first clause's place for the few seconds it is shown; then the info under the pointer; then the info on the keyboard focus; then the conventions.

Write the lines plainly, verb first where the control acts, in one voice for the whole window, and check each against what the control does rather than what it is called: a wrong line is worse than none. Consumer provides: the conventions as (verb, rest) pairs, an outcome clause while there is one, and each control's info line.
