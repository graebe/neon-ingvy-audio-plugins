# Ground

The animated window ground: the dot paper and grain as the surface of a slow, damped wave field that keeps the host's musical time. While the transport plays, every quarter note drops a ring just off a box's corner and each bar's downbeat a stronger one at the anchor, the corner nearest the window's centre; nothing rings while the transport is stopped. Rings spread as circles, bend around box corners, echo off box edges again and again, are half let out by the window border, interfere, and come to rest about ten seconds after the last ring (model, rules and parameters: Motion in the brand book). The ground is identical in every plugin and independent of the plugin's audio. Put a `canvas.ph-ground` as the first child of a `.ph-window.has-ground`; the window's other children stay above it. The static CSS ground stays underneath as the fallback and is pixel-identical to the canvas at rest.

**Consumer provides:** the canvas; the boxes that reflect and carry the emitters at their corners (by default every `.ph-panel`, `.ph-grid`, `.ph-ring`, `.ph-plot` and `[data-wave-source]` in the window, as rects relative to the canvas, passed to `setSources` and recomputed on resize); the rings, one `trigger(strength)` each, counted against the host's transport; and a Motion switch (`Toggle`) in the `Hint` bar, wired to `setEnabled`. In a plugin the beat is counted where the host's clock is, on the audio thread, from its position, tempo, time signature and play state, and each ring reaches the editor as a strength. A page that reads a transport itself ticks a `BeatClock` once a frame, which applies the same rule.

**API** (`components/ground.js`, global `UVGround`): `new Field(canvas, opts)`, `.setSources(rects)`, `.trigger(strength 0–1, at)` (`at`, optional `{x, y}`: drop it there instead of at the next emitter), `.setEnabled(bool)`, `.resize()`, `.destroy()`; `new BeatClock(trigger, opts)` with `.tick(position, bpm, numerator, denominator, playing)` (the position in quarter notes) and `.reset()`. Each ring calls `trigger(strength)`, 1 on a downbeat and `beat` (0.55, about half) on every other quarter note; pass `field.trigger.bind(field)`. A start, a loop or a seek rings at most once: the beat it lands on, never the beats it skipped.

**Timing.** The field steps on a timer at its frame rate (`fps`, 30), never on `requestAnimationFrame`, and is not paused when the page reports itself hidden: plugin hosts report their editor pages as hidden while they are on screen, and a ground clocked by animation frames never moves there. Where a host throttles the page's own timers as well, tick from a timer in a dedicated worker, which it does not throttle. The timer runs only while the field moves and stops at rest; the host stops it with `destroy()` when the window closes.

**Do:** drive it from the host's beat only, the same in every plugin; keep one Ground per window; let the timer stop when the field is at rest. Tune strength with `gain`, persistence with `tau` and `borderR`, ring size with `freq`, how sharp a ring stays with `visc`. **Don't:** drive it from the plugin's audio; add idle motion while the transport is stopped; run waves through panels or wells (they are opaque on purpose); raise `gain`, `tau` or `borderR` until the field reads as an effect (a border that reflects everything turns the window into boiling water); or use it in a window without a Motion switch. `prefers-reduced-motion` turns it off regardless.

In the preview: play runs a 120 BPM transport in 4/4 through the real `BeatClock` (the Beat LED lights on each ring), the signature switches the meter, and a click on the ground drops one downbeat where it lands.
