# Ground

The animated window ground: the dot paper and grain as the surface of a slow, damped wave field that keeps the host's musical time. While the transport plays, every quarter note makes every box edge and the window border emit a ring and each bar's downbeat a stronger one; nothing rings while the transport is stopped. Rings reflect off boxes and the border, interfere, and ring out over about 20 s (model, rules and parameters: Motion in the brand book). The ground is identical in every plugin and independent of the plugin's audio. Put a `canvas.ph-ground` as the first child of a `.ph-window.has-ground`; the window's other children stay above it. The static CSS ground stays underneath as the fallback and is pixel-identical to the canvas at rest.

**Consumer provides:** the canvas; the boxes that emit and reflect (by default every `.ph-panel`, `.ph-grid`, `.ph-ring`, `.ph-plot` and `[data-wave-source]` in the window, as rects relative to the canvas, passed to `setSources` and recomputed on resize); the rings, one `trigger(strength)` each, counted against the host's transport; and a Motion switch (`Toggle`) in the `Hint` bar, wired to `setEnabled`. In a plugin the beat is counted where the host's clock is, on the audio thread, from its position, tempo, time signature and play state, and each ring reaches the editor as a strength. A page that reads a transport itself ticks a `BeatClock` once a frame, which applies the same rule.

**API** (`components/ground.js`, global `UVGround`): `new Field(canvas, opts)`, `.setSources(rects)`, `.trigger(strength 0–1)`, `.setEnabled(bool)`, `.resize()`, `.destroy()`; `new BeatClock(trigger, opts)` with `.tick(position, bpm, numerator, denominator, playing)` (the position in quarter notes) and `.reset()`. Each ring calls `trigger(strength)`, 1 on a downbeat and `beat` (0.55, about half) on every other quarter note; pass `field.trigger.bind(field)`. A start, a loop or a seek rings at most once: the beat it lands on, never the beats it skipped.

**Timing.** The field steps on a timer at its frame rate (`fps`, 30), never on `requestAnimationFrame`, and is not paused when the page reports itself hidden: plugin hosts report their editor pages as hidden while they are on screen, and a ground clocked by animation frames never moves there. Where a host throttles the page's own timers as well, tick from a timer in a dedicated worker, which it does not throttle. The timer runs only while the field moves and stops at rest; the host stops it with `destroy()` when the window closes.

**Do:** drive it from the host's beat only, the same in every plugin; keep one Ground per window; let the timer stop when the field is at rest. Tune strength with `gain`, persistence with `tau`, ring size with `freq`. **Don't:** drive it from the plugin's audio; add idle motion while the transport is stopped; run waves through panels or wells (they are opaque on purpose); raise `gain` or `tau` until the field reads as an effect (a long `tau` under a steady beat fills the window); or use it in a window without a Motion switch. `prefers-reduced-motion` turns it off regardless.

In the preview: play runs a 120 BPM transport in 4/4 through the real `BeatClock` (the Beat LED lights on each ring), the signature switches the meter, and a click on the ground rings one downbeat.
