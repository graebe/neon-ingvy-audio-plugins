# Ring

A circle of segments, one per step, for the step count and the playhead. Two variants.

**Display** (`ph-ring`): unlit segments `ink-dim`, active `uv`, the current step `ink`, on a `line-100` track. The `readout` number sits in the centre with a `label` under it. Use the 240px ring once per window as the window's face, or the 120px variant next to a control row. Consumer provides: step count, active count, playhead index, the centre value and label. The display ring is not a control: the count is changed elsewhere (a knob or the grid).

**Editable** (`ph-ring edit`, 240px): the pattern itself on the window's face, editable where it is drawn. Each step is an annular wedge filling 80 % of its slot, the first slot starting at 12 o'clock and the rest following clockwise. Wedges are at least 24px deep, 34px at 16 steps; as the count rises the band narrows from its inner edge, so the ring never changes size. An off step is a `line-200` wedge, the rail; an on step lights `uv` from the inner edge outward, the lit depth being its amount and never under 5 %, so an on step always shows; a tie is a hollow `uv` outline with an arc across its middle, as on a `Step`. Lit wedges carry the `glow-led` halo. While the transport runs, the playhead is an `ink` dot just inside the band at the step being played. The centre keeps the `readout` and its `label`.

Click a wedge to toggle its step (Shift: tie) and sweep to paint that state across every wedge the pointer crosses; the hit band reaches 6px past either edge of the wedges, and the hole in the middle is not a button. To the keyboard the ring is its count, Length: arrows change it by one, Page Up and Page Down jump to the next Length detent (by a beat where there is none), Home and End go to 1 and the maximum; keyboard focus is `glow-focus` round the ring. The ring has no detent ticks: its angle is a position in the pattern, not a length. Consumer provides: the steps (on, tie, amount), the count and its range, the detents, the playhead and whether the transport runs, the centre value and label, the info string, and what a click, a sweep and a new count do. Use one per window, as its face; the `StepGrid` edits the same steps.
